The problem

Trade credit runs on guesswork

Across India, small shops buy stock on credit from distributors and pay later. Nobody has a reliable way to check whether a retailer is creditworthy first. There is no standard score for trade, so decisions rest on a sales rep's word, late payments pile up, and new partners are hard to judge.

Retailer · gets scored

“How do I show I'm financially responsible?”

A shop owner with years of good payments but no paperwork to prove it. New suppliers won't give credit to a shop they can't check.

Supplier · checks the score

“Can I trust this retailer?”

A distributor's credit lead who needs a fast answer on each retailer's risk that they can defend, instead of relying on gut feel.

    Four principles guided every screen

    Trust through transparency

    Always show what we see, why the score is what it is, and who has looked.

    Guidance, not punishment

    Every low factor comes with a next step. Never a dead end.

    Readable at a glance

    Numbers, bands and icons first. Short words. Thumb-reachable actions on a budget phone.

    Serious, not cold

    The calm, precise tone of a bank, with the plain language of a shopkeeper.

    The key idea

    A score you earn, not one you're handed

    Bank data shows cash flow, not whether a shop pays its suppliers. So a new retailer doesn't get a number on day one. The score grows up in three stages, and the app is honest about how sure it is at each one.

    Facts, not a score

    The retailer sees progress toward a first score, and suppliers see verified facts from their bank data. The first score needs 3 verified credits from at least 2 suppliers over 60+ days.

    A range, clearly labelled

    The first score is a range, like 62–74, marked “Early” so suppliers know it is still settling. It narrows to one number as more credits are verified.

    One number, with its confidence

    After 10 closed credits or 6 months of history, the retailer gets a single score out of 100, a band, a confidence level and a breakdown of five factors, each with a next step.

    What changed and why

    Three decisions I reversed during design

    Working through the flows in detail showed gaps in the first plan. I kept the early boards as they were, so the reasoning stays visible.

    1. No score on day one

      BeforeA score in about a minute, straight from bank data.
      AfterBuilding, then an early range, then an established number.

      Why: a day-one number would claim a certainty the data can't support.

    2. Credits confirmed by both sides

      BeforeSuppliers upload a monthly payment ledger.
      AfterThe supplier records each credit, the retailer confirms it within 48 hours, and the supplier confirms payment within 7 days.

      Why: a one-sided ledger can be wrong or gamed. Two confirmations make every data point trustworthy and give retailers a say.

    3. Past credits count

      BeforeHistory starts on the day the retailer signs up.
      AfterRetailers add credits from before they joined, and the supplier verifies each one from a WhatsApp link.

      Why: shops with years of good history reach a first score in weeks instead of months.

    Trust & consent

    The retailer decides who sees what

    Many shop owners distrust apps with their bank data, so consent is spelled out in plain words before anything is shared.

    • No surprises: before linking a bank through India's Account Aggregator system, the app shows exactly what will be requested, and what it will never do.
    • Supplier asks, retailer approves: each request lists what the supplier will and will never see.
    • Time-bound and revocable: access lasts 30 or 90 days, can be stopped any time, and every view is logged, in line with India's DPDP Act.

    The approval screen works: try switching between 30 and 90 days.

    Supplier dashboard

    From a score to a credit decision you can defend

    Suppliers work at a desk, so their side is a light-themed web dashboard. The overview shows who needs attention, and each retailer's report turns the score into a suggested credit limit.

    More of the app

    Guidance, and verification without an app

    Visual identity

    Four directions, one chosen: Midnight

    I explored four brand directions. A study of how leading fintech apps make money feel calm and premium led to Midnight: a dark interface with a single violet accent, Plus Jakarta Sans, and a light theme for the supplier dashboard. The score got its own set of components, from the gauge to the stage indicators.

    What's next

    Testing it with the people it's for

    The hi-fi design is complete. Next is a usability test with 5 retailers and 5 suppliers, then another round of design. Targets will be set from that baseline. The test will measure:

    • For retailers: how many get from invite to first score, how long it takes, and where they drop off at the bank consent step.
    • For suppliers: time from request to credit decision, and success at setting a credit limit.
    • For the business: fewer overdue payments among scored retailers, and suppliers still using it after 90 days.
